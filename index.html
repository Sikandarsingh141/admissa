Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to the most common questions about studying abroad

The total cost varies by country. For MBBS in Russia, expect $30,000-40,000 for the entire course (including tuition and living). In Kyrgyzstan, it's approximately $25,000-35,000. Philippines costs around $28,000-38,000 total. This is significantly cheaper than private medical colleges in India which can cost ₹1-2 crore.
It depends on the country and program. For MBBS in Russia, Kyrgyzstan, and Philippines, IELTS is not mandatory. However, for countries like Canada, UK, USA, and Australia, you'll need IELTS (minimum 6.0-6.5) or TOEFL for admission. Some universities may waive this requirement if you've completed your previous education in English medium.
Yes! In Canada, UK, and Australia, international students can work up to 20 hours per week during semesters and full-time during breaks. In USA, on-campus work is allowed. However, in Russia, Kyrgyzstan, and Philippines, part-time work opportunities are limited, so it's best to focus on studies and rely on planned funding.
Absolutely! All medical universities we recommend are NMC (National Medical Commission) approved and WHO listed. This means you can practice medicine in India after clearing FMGE/NExT exam. We only partner with verified, recognized institutions to ensure your degree is valid and accepted worldwide.
Visa processing time varies by country. Canada takes 4-8 weeks, UK takes 3-4 weeks, Australia takes 4-6 weeks, and USA takes 2-8 weeks. For Russia and Kyrgyzstan, the process is faster at 2-4 weeks. We recommend starting your visa application at least 3 months before your intended travel date to avoid any last-minute stress.
Basic documents include: 10th & 12th mark sheets, passport, photos, NEET scorecard (for MBBS), birth certificate, and medical fitness certificate. For postgraduate programs, you'll need bachelor's degree transcripts, LORs, SOP, and test scores (GRE/GMAT if required). We provide a detailed checklist and help you compile everything correctly.
